# Lung opacity
aliases: airspace opacification, consolidation, infiltrate, increased density, air bronchogram
source: https://radiopaedia.org/articles/airspace-opacification

A lung opacity is any area of the lung that looks whiter than normal on a radiograph. It is a descriptive finding, not a diagnosis: the cause is suggested by its pattern, distribution and the clinical context.

Airspace opacity (consolidation) means the alveoli are filled with fluid, pus, blood or cells; air bronchograms inside the opacity support an airspace process. Common causes include pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, haemorrhage and, less often, tumour.

Comparison with prior imaging and correlation with symptoms such as fever, cough or breathlessness are essential. When pneumonia is treated in an older patient or a smoker, a follow-up radiograph after several weeks is often used to confirm resolution and exclude an underlying lesion.
