# Pleural effusion
aliases: effusion, pleural fluid, costophrenic angle blunting, meniscus sign
source: https://radiopaedia.org/articles/pleural-effusion

On an upright frontal radiograph, the earliest sign of a pleural effusion is blunting of the lateral costophrenic angle, often with a curved upper margin (meniscus sign). Small effusions may only be visible on the lateral view, where the posterior costophrenic angle blunts first.

On a supine or semi-erect film, fluid layers posteriorly and may only appear as a hazy increase in density over the lower hemithorax, so small effusions are easily missed. A large effusion can push the mediastinum towards the opposite side.

Ultrasound is more sensitive than radiography for small effusions and is used to guide drainage. Bilateral effusions are frequent in heart failure; a unilateral effusion raises the question of infection, malignancy or pulmonary embolism and is correlated with the clinical picture.
