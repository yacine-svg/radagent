# Cardiomegaly
fr: Cardiomégalie
aliases: enlarged heart, cardiac enlargement, cardiothoracic ratio, enlarged cardiac silhouette
source: https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cardiomegaly

On an upright posteroanterior (PA) chest radiograph, cardiomegaly is suggested when the cardiothoracic ratio is above 0.5: the widest transverse diameter of the heart is more than half the widest internal diameter of the thorax.

Anteroposterior (AP) and supine radiographs magnify the heart because it lies further from the detector, so a ratio slightly above 0.5 on an AP or bedside film is not reliable on its own. A pericardial effusion can also enlarge the cardiac silhouette without true cardiac enlargement.

Common associations include heart failure, cardiomyopathy, valvular disease and long-standing hypertension. The radiograph only suggests enlargement: echocardiography is the usual next step to assess chamber size and function, and comparison with prior radiographs helps to judge change.
